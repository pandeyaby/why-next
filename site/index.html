Why Next?

See what your child is ready to learn next, and why each step comes first.

Around age eleven, homework turns into algebra, chemistry and physics, and stops looking like anything most of us remember. Why Next? sets out secondary maths and science as small, connected steps, each with a plain reason. You don’t need to be the expert to help. You just need to see what comes next.

The tricky years

The years when it gets hard to help

When a teenager is stuck, it’s hard to tell whether the problem is today’s topic or something from two years ago. Why Next? traces every topic back to the steps it rests on, so you can find the real starting point together, without guessing and without a lecture.

How it works for families

Four small steps, no expertise needed

Use it for five minutes after dinner or an hour at the weekend. There is nothing to install and nothing to sign up for.

  1. Step 1Start from what they can do

    Answer a few quick questions, or open the map and browse together. No tests, no accounts.

  2. Step 2See what’s within reach

    The map picks out the topics that build directly on what they already know: sensible next steps, not a random worksheet.

  3. Step 3Ask “why?” and get an answer

    Every link says what carries over from the step before. “Why do I need this?” finally has a real answer.

  4. Step 4Talk about it at the kitchen table

    Each subject area has a plain summary and a quick question to try. Five minutes, no worksheet, no pressure.

What we believe

“Why do I need this?” is a good question

It usually sounds like a complaint. We think it’s the best question in the room. Every topic at secondary school is there because something later depends on it, and every topic leans on something earlier. When you can see those links, the work stops feeling like a list of hoops and starts to look like a path.

That’s the whole idea: show the path, explain each step, and let families walk it together.

  • Reasons, not rules

    No step appears out of nowhere. Each link says, in one line, what it reuses.

  • Written for families

    Plain words a parent can follow, so you can help without dusting off a textbook.

  • Open to everyone

    Free, openly licensed data. Schools, tutors and app makers can all build on it.

  • Honest about limits

    It’s a map, not a verdict on your child. And it’s a draft that hasn’t yet been checked by teachers.

FAQ

Questions parents ask

Is this a course or an app?

Neither. It’s a map: topics, the links between them, and a reason for each link. Use it alongside any school, textbook, tutor or app.

Does it collect anything about my child?

No. There are no accounts, cookies or analytics, and the demo on this page runs entirely in your browser. Close the tab and it’s gone.

How accurate is the demo?

It’s a friendly estimate, not a test. Three answers can’t tell us everything, so we show what follows directly from your answers separately from what we’ve estimated by age, and we always say which is which. The topics and reasons themselves come straight from the dataset.

Who made it, and has it been checked?

It builds on the open Marble Skill Taxonomy, which covers the primary years, and extends it to age 18. Every new topic and link was reviewed by its author and the dataset passes automated checks, but it has not yet been reviewed by teachers. If you spot something wrong, please say so on GitHub.

Which country’s curriculum does it follow?

It’s organised by age rather than by one country’s school years, so families anywhere can use it. Where the underlying data mentions English Key Stages, this page shows the matching ages instead (hover for the Key Stage and the approximate US grades). Some science topics carry US high-school (NGSS) codes, and standards are only attached where a real code exists.

My child is ahead in some things and behind in others. Is that a problem?

Not at all, that’s normal. The demo works one subject at a time for exactly this reason. Treat the results as a starting point for a conversation, not an assessment.
